The tradition

Emeralds in jewellery.

For as long as there have been crowns, there have been emeralds set into them, the green of rank, renewal and quiet power.

Emeralds have been treasured across nearly every civilisation that prized colour, from the ancient mines worked for the pharaohs, through the Mughal courts who inscribed them, to the crowns of Europe and the vaults of the great modern houses. For thousands of years the deep green has signified status, love and continuity, worn by those able to command the rarest thing the earth offers.

After decades in the shadow of the colourless diamond, the emerald is once again at the centre of things, on the red carpet, in the engagement ring and heading the season’s high-jewellery collections. Designers are reaching for colour and no colour carries the authority of a fine Colombian green.

Its pull has never been only visual. The emerald has long been associated with the heart, with renewal, devotion and clarity of thought and is still chosen as much for what it signifies as for how it looks, a meaning that reaches back to antiquity.

And increasingly, the finest emeralds are regarded the way collectors have always regarded exceptional coloured stones: as a portable store of value. Top-colour, single-origin Colombian material is genuinely scarce and that scarcity has drawn a new kind of buyer, one who sees a place for a great stone alongside the more familiar assets of a serious portfolio.

At the source

Chosen before they are cut.

The stones we offer begin as rough, at the mines of Muzo, Chivor and Coscuez. We acquire emerald in parcels at source and every parcel is assessed by our own gemmologist and cut by our lapidary, so the journey from crystal to finished stone happens under a single, accountable eye.

For collectors, this opens a rarer proposition than buying finished stones alone: to acquire a vetted rough parcel and have it cut to order. It is the closest most buyers will come to the mine itself.
